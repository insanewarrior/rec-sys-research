\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:setup:data}

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} lists the seven datasets used. They are
chosen to span two regimes of intensity signal: discrete,
low-entropy explicit ratings ($1$--$5$) on MovieLens-100K and
MovieLens-1M~\citep{harper2015movielens},
Amazon Digital Music 5-core and Amazon Office Products 5-core~\citep{mcauley2015amazon,he2016amazonups};
and continuous, heavy-tailed implicit hours-played on three Steam
Reviews subsamples~\citep{pathak2017steam,wan2018steamreviews}
($3$k, $8$k, $15$k users; fixed seed 2020). All
datasets are 5-core filtered ($|\text{interactions}_u|, |\text{interactions}_i| \ge 5$).
The split is per-user chronological leave-one-out: the latest interaction
forms the test target, the second-latest is held out as validation, the
remainder is training.

\subsection{Training and hyperparameter optimisation}
\label{sec:setup:hpo}

Every (dataset, model) pair receives an identical hyperparameter budget:
$25$ Optuna~\citep{akiba2019optuna} trials with the TPE sampler,
optimising validation NDCG@10 with $10$ epochs per trial (short HPO).
The search space for SASRec and the three IA-SASRec variants is the
same on the SASRec hyperparameters ($n_{\mathrm{layers}}{\in}[1,3]$,
$n_{\mathrm{heads}}{\in}\{1,2,4\}$, $\mathrm{hidden\_size}, \mathrm{inner\_size}$,
dropouts, learning rate), with one extra categorical for IA-SASRec
selecting $\texttt{intensity\_norm}\in\{\texttt{log1p\_minmax},\texttt{minmax},\texttt{zscore},\texttt{none}\}$.
The learnable $\lambda$'s are model parameters, not hyperparameters.
The final fit uses each model's HPO-best configuration, trains for up
to $50$ epochs with early stopping on validation NDCG@10 (patience $5$),
and is repeated across five seeds.
Test metrics come from a single pass using the best-on-validation
checkpoint; validation data is never folded back into training.

This protocol gives the IA-SASRec variants strictly more
hyperparameter freedom than SASRec (one extra categorical at no extra
trial cost), and identical training budget per seed. Under this
protocol, an under-tuning explanation would require a configuration
outside the shared search space.

\subsection{Evaluation: dual-criterion significance with a per-seed stability check}
\label{sec:setup:eval}

We report NDCG@10, Recall@10, and MRR@10 at the standard $k{=}10$
sequential-recommendation cutoff~\citep{kang2018sasrec,sun2019bert4rec}.
Under our leave-one-out full-ranking protocol --- exactly one held-out
positive per user, ranked against the full item catalogue --- Hit@K
coincides with Recall@K and Precision@K equals Recall@K$/K$, so the
three metrics we report are the non-redundant trio (set-membership,
reciprocal rank, log-discounted rank); Hit and Precision are computed
but omitted as they carry no information beyond Recall.

A standard single-seed table is underpowered for small effect sizes:
training stochasticity and test-population sampling are both large at
the scale of these datasets~\citep{ferrari2019,sun2020daisy}. We
report a metric difference as robust only when two independent
hypothesis tests, each sensitive to a different source of variance,
both reject at $\alpha{=}0.05$, and a per-seed stability check
confirms that the per-user test is not dominated by a single seed:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Seed-level paired $t$-test on the $n{=}5$ per-seed
test metrics ($\mathrm{df}{=}4$, two-sided $\alpha{=}0.05$). Unit of
variation: random training seed. Pairing is by seed so the same
data split sits on both sides of the comparison.
\item[(ii)] Per-user paired bootstrap~\citep{efron1993bootstrap} ($B{=}2{,}000$ resamples)
on aligned per-user metric differences pooled across seeds, with
percentile $95\%$ CI and two-sided $p = 2 \min(\Pr[\bar d{\le}0], \Pr[\bar d{\ge}0])$
floored at $1/B$. Unit of variation: test user.
\item[(iii)] Per-seed CI sign-agreement majority: a stability
check, not an independent hypothesis test. We re-run the bootstrap
of (ii) independently within each seed and require $\ge 3$ of $5$
per-seed $95\%$ CIs to sit entirely on the same side of zero. This
guards against (ii) being driven by a single lucky seed.
\end{enumerate}

Cells in our main results table are bolded only when (i) and (ii) both
reject at $\alpha{=}0.05$ and the stability check holds. For
full-vocabulary scoring rather than sampled top-$k$, see the cautions
in~\citep{krichene2020sampled}.

\subsection{Implementation and reproducibility}
\label{sec:setup:repro}

The model is built on top of RecBole~\citep{zhao2021recbole}
(PyTorch~\citep{paszke2019pytorch} back-end), with an
additional intensity column threaded through the shared data pipeline
so that baselines and intensity-aware variants run on identical inputs.
All artefacts needed to reproduce the analysis (code, checkpoints,
per-seed evaluation outputs, and per-user metric tables) are released
alongside the paper.
